Day 2 of 100: Milky Way Calendar.

If you've ever driven two hours to a dark site and found a full Moon washing out the sky, this one's for you.

It's an iPhone app that rates every night for the next 12 months for seeing the Milky Way core from wherever you are. A night only counts when three things line up:
→ the sky is fully dark (sun 18° below the horizon)
→ the Moon is down
→ the galactic core is actually up and reasonably high

You get a year of calendars shaded Great / Good / Fair, a bar chart of good nights per month, and for any night the exact window, which way to face and how high to look.

From the Bay Area the season starts with a pre-dawn window on April 2 and peaks in May and June. Pick Atacama in Chile instead and the calendar fills up.

The sun, moon and core math is reused from AstroScout, my astrophotography planner, so today was mostly about the planning logic and the calendar itself.

No account, no tracking. Everything runs on the phone.
