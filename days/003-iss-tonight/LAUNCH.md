Day 3 of 100: ISS Tonight.

The International Space Station is one of the brightest things in the night sky, and most people have never knowingly seen it. The trick is knowing when to look.

ISS Tonight tells you the next time you can see it from where you are, in plain words:
→ 6:18 PM: appears in the south-southwest, low on the horizon
→ 6:22 PM: climbs to 15° up (about two fists), then fades into Earth's shadow

It lists every visible pass for the next 10 days, rates how bright each one will be, and gives you a calendar reminder with a 10-minute alarm.

The orbit math (SGP4, the Earth's shadow, sun position) is ported from SatellitePaglu, my iPhone satellite tracker, so today was about the pass logic and making the answer readable at a glance. The orbit data comes fresh from CelesTrak each time.

It's a web page, so it works on any phone. No app, no account, no tracking. Your location stays in your browser.

If you're near London, the evening of Nov 16 has a very bright pass, 57° up.
