      What is a Starlink train, and how do I see one?
      • SpaceX launches a few dozen Starlink satellites at a time. For the first days they fly one behind the other, low and bright, so you see a line of dots gliding across the sky over a few minutes.
      • Within one to three weeks they climb to their working orbits and spread around the planet. The train is gone; you'd only spot single satellites. This page checks how bunched up each recent launch still is.
      • You can only see them around dusk and dawn: your sky has to be dark while the satellites, a few hundred km up, are still in sunlight.
      • Face the direction it appears from a minute early. "20° up" is about two fists held at arm's length above the horizon.
      Why a calendar reminder and not an email?

      Sending email needs a server and a mail account, and this is a single page with no backend and no sign-up. A calendar event with a 15-minute alarm does the same job: it pings you on your phone the evening of the pass. Times can shift by a few minutes as the satellites raise their orbits, so open this page again that afternoon to check.
