Day 6 of 100: Moon Shot Planner.

You've seen the photo: a giant full moon sitting right on top of a famous tower. It's not luck and it's not Photoshop. It's geometry, and someone stood in exactly the right place at exactly the right minute.

Moon Shot Planner works that out for you. Pick a landmark, slide how far back you want to stand, and it lists every moonrise in the next 60 days where the moon lines up with the top. Tap one and it shows the spot to stand on a map, which way to face, how lit the moon is and what the light will be like.

The further back you stand, the bigger the moon looks next to the building. From 3 km, the Transamerica Pyramid's tip is 4.9° up, and the best shot this month is November 24, the full moon at 5:22 PM, from near Alamo Square.

The fun part was the small stuff. Refraction lifts the moon by a third of its own width near the horizon, and the moon shifts up to a degree depending on where you stand. Get either wrong and you miss the top.

Built from scratch today; positions come from the open-source Astronomy Engine, running in your browser. No account, no tracking.
