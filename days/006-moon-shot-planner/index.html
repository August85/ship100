    How it works

    From where you stand, a landmark's top sits at a small angle above the horizon. The further back you go, the lower that angle and the bigger the moon looks next to it. For every moonrise in the next 60 days, the planner finds the minute the moon's centre climbs to that angle, reads the direction it's in, and puts you on the opposite side of the landmark at your chosen distance.

    Moon and sun positions come from Astronomy Engine, worked out in your browser, with parallax, refraction and Earth's curvature included. Times are good to about a minute.

    It assumes flat, open ground at the landmark's base height. Hills, trees, buildings and water aren't in its data, so check the spot on the map before you go, and slide the distance if it lands somewhere you can't stand.
