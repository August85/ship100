Day 8 of 100: Rocket Launch Feed.

If you live in Southern California, you've probably seen it at least once: a glowing, spreading "jellyfish" in the sky just after sunset. It's a Falcon 9 from Vandenberg, and its exhaust is still catching sunlight 150 km up while you're standing in the dark.

The hard part is knowing which launches will do that from where you are. Most launch sites list a time and a pad, and that's it.

Rocket Launch Feed lists the upcoming Vandenberg launches and, for your town, says whether you'll see a twilight plume, a night-time engine flame, a daytime contrail, or nothing. It tells you which way to look and how high, has a countdown and a calendar button, and lists viewing spots from Lompoc to Point Dume to Joshua Tree.

Under the hood it works out where the sun is for you and for the pad at liftoff, then checks whether the rocket clears your horizon once you allow for Earth's curve. It's the same sun-position library I used for Day 6's Moon Shot Planner. The launch data is from Launch Library 2 by The Space Devs.

No account, no tracking, no keys.
