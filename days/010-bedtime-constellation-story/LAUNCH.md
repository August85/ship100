Day 10 of 100: Bedtime Constellation Story.

My favourite bedtime trick with small kids is to step outside, point at a few stars and say "that's a bear". It works every time, as long as I know which bear is actually up there tonight, and remember the story.

So I built a page that does the remembering. Pick your city and bedtime. It works out which constellation is well placed in your sky at that hour, draws it the way it's actually turned tonight, tells you where to look ("north-east, high up"), and gives you a short story to read aloud. Or tap "Read it to me" and let the browser do the voice.

There are 16 constellations, northern and southern sky, and the stories are written for 3 to 6 year olds: five short paragraphs that end in goodnight. Some are Greek, but where another culture had a gentler tale I used that one: the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl across the Milky Way, Maui's magic fishhook, the Teapot that pours out stars.

Same idea as the earlier sky days (Meteor Shower Countdown, ISS Tonight): work out what's actually up over your house, then make it simple. This time the audience is just much smaller.

No account, no tracking, works offline once loaded.
