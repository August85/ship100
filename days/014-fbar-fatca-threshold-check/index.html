How it's worked out

  • FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) is due if the highest balances of all your foreign accounts, added together, went over $10,000 at any point in the year. It counts accounts you only have signing authority on, too. It's filed online with FinCEN (BSA E-Filing), not with your tax return. Due 15 April, with an automatic extension to 15 October.
  • Form 8938 (FATCA) goes with your Form 1040. The limits depend on filing status and where you live: $50k at year end or $75k at any time for single filers in the US; $100k / $150k if married filing jointly; $200k / $300k and $400k / $600k if you live abroad. You only file it if you have to file a tax return. Accounts you can sign on but don't own are left out.
  • "At any time" for Form 8938 is worked out here as the sum of each account's highest balance, the cautious reading most preparers use.
  • Exchange rate: both forms use the Treasury Reporting Rates of Exchange for 31 December of the tax year, loaded live from fiscaldata.treasury.gov. For a year that hasn't ended, the latest quarterly rate is used and marked as provisional.
  • Grey areas: PPF, EPF, NPS and insurance policies with a cash value are included by default, since most preparers count them. Untick "Count" on a row to see the result without it.
  • Joint accounts: for the FBAR, each owner counts the whole balance. For Form 8938, married couples filing jointly count a shared account once. Other cases have their own rules; check the Form 8938 instructions.
  • Missing a filing can carry steep penalties. If you're close to a line, or you've missed past years, talk to a cross-border tax preparer.
